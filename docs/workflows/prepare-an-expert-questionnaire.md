# Prepare an expert questionnaire

Use when another person holds information needed for a decision. Produce a readable draft answerable without the originating conversation. Preparing it does not send, schedule or store it externally.

Start with the decision, known context, intended recipient or role, and gaps they may know about. Reuse supplied information. Ask the caller only for missing context changing the draft, such as which recipient may receive sensitive details. Do not demand domain answers the caller is trying to obtain.

Identify what must be known to decide and who can reasonably supply it. Distinguish facts, constraints, preferences and approval requests. Split recipients into clearly addressed sections or separate drafts when knowledge differs. Do not imply authority beyond a stated role.

Write the purpose, enough context for independent interpretation, and how answers will be used. Include deadlines and effort estimates only when supplied; otherwise omit them. Invent neither identity nor expertise nor organizational commitments.

Prioritize questions changing the decision. Ask one fact or decision per numbered question with an answer space directly below. Split compound questions. Add rationale only where misreading is likely. Permit partial answers, unknown answers, corrections and delegation. Avoid unnecessary sensitive information.

Read as the recipient: can they understand the situation, recognize their knowledge and answer without this chat? Check every essential gap has a question and no question assumes a disputed answer. Mark optional questions visibly.

Return the draft in the conversation unless an exact file destination is authorized; apply ordinary destination/path rules for a save. Do not transmit it, create reminders or start collection automatically. Later answers remain attributed evidence with uncertainty and contradictions. They do not authorize spending, publication or system changes. Use them in subsequent assessment or an agreed plan only within that task's authority.

## Flow and limits

Decision and recipient -> identify knowledge gaps -> prepare recipient-ready questions -> check assumptions and disclosure -> return the draft. No installed skill, subagent, research service or external delivery is required. The optional [Ask me skill](../../skills/ask-me/SKILL.md) can help clarify the decision before drafting; its use does not authorize sending or collection.

Read the linked source for its complete interview and capture boundaries. Stop and ask only when missing decision, recipient or shareable context would materially change the draft. A complete brief needs no additional interview.

## Synthetic worked example

### Draft for the stockroom coordinator

We are deciding whether to pilot a shared equipment checkout desk. We need to understand current tracking and constraints before choosing a pilot. Please answer what you know; partial answers and "unknown" are useful. You may name a better person for any item. Questions are ordered by priority.

1. Where are equipment checkouts currently recorded?

   Answer:

2. Which equipment category has the most frequent tracking problems, if known?

   Answer:

3. Is there an existing rule governing who may borrow equipment?

   Answer, with a document pointer if available:

4. What constraint would prevent a small checkout-desk pilot?

   Answer:

Optional: Is there a decision-relevant issue these questions miss?

Answer:

This example asserts no deadline, quantified loss or permission to launch the pilot.

## Reusable invocation prompt

Prepare an expert questionnaire for [recipient or role] so we can decide [decision]. Known context: [facts and evidence]. Missing information: [gaps]. Reuse supplied context and ask me only missing recipient, decision or context details materially affecting the draft. Give the recipient enough context, prioritize one fact or decision per question, include answer spaces, and permit unknown, partial or delegated answers. Invent no deadline or expertise. Return a draft; do not send it or treat answers as action consent.
