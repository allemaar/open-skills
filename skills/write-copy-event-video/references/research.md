# Event copy: techniques and evidence boundaries

Research bank version: 2026-09-29. These are reusable starting points to refine against the audience and offering. They do not establish an optimal creative or a conversion guarantee. Recheck platform recommendations before citing them as current. Read original sources when making a new precise factual claim.

| Technique or decision | Use | Evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Start with a concrete situation the intended audience can recognise | Practical copy heuristic. Relevance depends on audience evidence; familiarity is not proof that everyone has the problem. |
| Specific curiosity gap | Pose a useful question, then reveal enough to make the invitation credible | Kang and colleagues studied curiosity, reward circuitry and memory in a trivia-learning setting. It does not establish ticket-sales uplift or validate a specific hook. [Kang et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19619181/) |
| Tangible benefit and demonstration | Show what the session explains or demonstrates; connect it to a real use | Google's ABCD guidance covers attention, branding, connection and direction. It supports clear, focused video advertising, not claims of guaranteed course outcomes. [Google ABCD guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en) |
| Approachable first step | Clarify entry level and prerequisites when the programme is introductory | Practical heuristic. May reduce uncertainty about fit; do not infantilise the audience or conceal prerequisites. |
| Credible proof | Use an authentic demonstration, relevant qualification or sourced result | Evidence must belong to this offer. A prestigious name, invented quote or unrelated statistic is not proof. |
| Single primary CTA | Make the next action consistent and reachable | Eventbrite's event-copy guidance supports a clear call to action. Multiple placements of the same action can be useful; competing asks divide the message. [Eventbrite copywriting guide](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Copywriting-Guide-For-Events.pdf) |
| Short first test | Start with one story and enough room for comprehension | TikTok's cited guide recommends roughly 21–34 seconds in its advertising context. LinkedIn's cited tips recommend 15–30 seconds for awareness campaigns. Neither is a universal optimum for organic event videos. [TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/guides/video-advertising-guide), [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/linkedin-ads/your-one-stop-shop-for-linkedin-video-ad-tips) |
| 30–35 / 45 / 60 seconds | 30–35 for a first invitation test; 45 for a real example; 60 for substantial explanation | Local creative hypothesis, not a research result. Choose length by useful content and measured delivery. |

## Responsible persuasion

Offer a concrete reason to care and a credible next step. Use true dates or verified deadlines for urgency. Never manufacture scarcity, social proof, guarantees, fear of replacement, or consequences of not buying. A beginner's uncertainty is a reason to explain fit, not to shame them.

## Conclusions, creative choices and production preferences

The practical synthesis is a focused invitation: recognise a relevant situation, reveal a useful possibility, establish fit and credibility, then offer one next action. This is a flexible writing pattern, not a fixed sequence scientifically shown to sell tickets. A result-first opening or a brief authentic demonstration can be equally appropriate. The provisional duration bands remain test hypotheses.

Campaign choices such as energetic invitation, plain native language, informal address, short sentences, deliberate repetition only, and copy approval before visual variations are creative preferences. Record the caller's actual choices and their scope. Simplicity should preserve specificity, meaning and momentum; it should not flatten the message into generic boilerplate. Convey positive agency through what someone could use or explore, without exaggerated promises. Keep a teaching excerpt subordinate to the reason to attend when the purpose is event promotion.

Production preferences are another layer: reusable local motion graphics, paid assets only for justified special flair, and one coherent visual language can be requested constraints. They are not findings from the psychology or platform sources above. Pass those constraints to design, including rejected discontinuities between unrelated 3D scenes and cards; do not perform production in this writing skill. Approve copy first, audition voice before full narration, then fit the visual treatment to the chosen voice.

## Repetition and testing

Assign every sentence a job. Remove a second sentence that adds no information, proof, emotional progression, or useful recall. If the closing line echoes the opening, record why. Repetition across overlays and speech is optional emphasis, not an excuse to display paragraphs.

Compare one variable under comparable conditions. Preselect a metric related to the goal, such as relevant event-page visits or registrations; retention helps locate drop-offs. Record distribution, audience, time window, actual sample sizes, and uncertainty. A small difference is not automatically a winner. Do not turn a channel's historical performance into a promise for the next event.
