# Optional impact versus effort

Use to triage candidate actions under limited capacity. Ranking is a starting point for sequencing, not authority to cut mandatory work.

- Define the outcome and time horizon that impact means, the effort unit and available capacity. Include delivery, coordination, migration and continuing support costs. Keep estimates and confidence visible; unknown effort is not zero.
- Compare on consistent scales. If drawing a matrix, put effort left-to-right from low to high and impact bottom-to-top from low to high; explain the dividing thresholds. Use ranges or qualitative placement when precision is unsupported.
- High-impact/low-effort items are candidate quick wins; high/high items need investment choices. Low-impact items may be deferred or avoided, but check obligations, dependencies, deadlines and enabling work first. Automation and delegation have costs and need a separate feasibility check.
- Test whether plausible estimate changes reverse the ranking. Sequence against dependencies and actual capacity, explaining any override. If evidence cannot support a ranking, return the missing inputs instead of invented scores. Do not run multiple frameworks merely to seek agreement.

Return candidate priorities, a feasible proposed sequence, exceptions and sensitive assumptions. Feed them into the existing assessment verdict; do not execute them.
