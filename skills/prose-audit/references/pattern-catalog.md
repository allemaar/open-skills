# Prose inflation pattern catalog

Use this catalog to recognize load. Do not dump it into the report. Group
several instances when they share one cause.

## Answer delay

- Throat-clearing: "It is important to note that".
- Question restatement when the question is already clear.
- Context preamble that does not change the answer.
- Meta-announcement: "Here is a comprehensive breakdown".
- Scope ceremony: claiming several dimensions before naming them.
- Permission narration: "Let us now explore".

## Semantic repetition

- Immediate paraphrase of a plain sentence.
- Restatement loops using "in other words" or "put simply".
- Definition followed by an equivalent definition.
- Example followed by an explanation of the obvious example.
- Opening thesis repeated in the conclusion.
- Bullets repeated in a takeaway, summary, and closing paragraph.
- Consequence chains whose clauses are synonyms.
- The same claim repeated under different headings.

## Manufactured structure

- Headings for single sentences.
- Numbering where order has no meaning.
- Nested bullets that explain their parent without adding a distinction.
- Fake taxonomies with overlapping categories.
- Forced symmetry, especially equal-sized sections without equal substance.
- Decorative tables, checklists, phases, pillars, dimensions, or acronyms.
- Separate summary, takeaway, bottom line, and conclusion for one short answer.

## Inflated diction

- Nominalization: "conduct an evaluation" instead of "evaluate".
- Verb dilution: "serves to demonstrate" instead of "shows".
- Long connectors: "due to the fact that" instead of "because".
- Formal substitutions: "utilize" instead of "use".
- Existential openings: "there are several reasons".
- Passive authority fog: "it has been observed".
- Abstract noun fog: alignment, enablement, transformation, orchestration.
- Decorative adjective, verb, or noun stacks.
- Near-duplicate pairs such as "goals and objectives".

## Stock rhetoric

- Litotes used without a deliberate restrained or ironic effect.
- "Not X, but Y" when X and Y are not real alternatives.
- "More than X" transformation claims.
- Triads added for cadence rather than distinctions.
- Paired abstractions: risks and considerations, context and nuance.
- Generic profundity: "at its core", "everything is interconnected".
- Journey, transformation, future-shaping, and balance metaphors.
- LinkedIn cadence, manufactured revelation, and engagement bait.

## Qualification without precision

- Hedge stacking: may, perhaps, somewhat, generally, in some cases.
- "It depends" without naming the deciding variables.
- "No one-size-fits-all answer" without the actual decision rule.
- Possibility phrased as support: "could lead to improved outcomes".
- Vague attribution: experts agree, studies show, observers suggest.
- Confidence smoothing that hides known, inferred, estimated, and unknown.

Necessary uncertainty is payload. Remove duplicate or non-specific hedges, not
conditions that bound the truth of a claim.

## Empty balance

- Automatic pros and cons where evidence is unequal.
- A fringe objection given equal space.
- "The reality is nuanced" without naming the nuance.
- Middle-position reflex: the answer must lie between both sides.
- Both-and synthesis when the choices conflict.
- A weak counterargument invented only to be defeated.

## Detail without signal

- Generic examples with no mechanism, name, measurement, or distinction.
- Several interchangeable examples after one establishes the pattern.
- Placeholder stakeholders: users, teams, leaders, organizations.
- Unmeasured improvement claims: significant, powerful, impactful.
- Decorative technical terms: architecture, ecosystem, paradigm.
- Obvious causal explanation.
- Generic advice: monitor results and adjust as needed.

## Unnecessary completeness

- Answering adjacent questions the Handler did not ask.
- Rare edge cases before the main rule.
- History, future trends, audience variants, or frequently asked questions added
  by default.
- Alternatives without ranking.
- A caution section unrelated to a realistic risk.
- Unrequested implementation, testing, deployment, or architecture detail.

## Reader and tone padding

- Politeness openers and praise before the answer.
- Reassurance without evidence of confusion or distress.
- Imagined objections: "you may be wondering".
- Attention commands: remember, keep in mind, it is important to understand.
- Motivational endings and unsolicited follow-up offers.
- Corporate filler: leverage, empower, optimize, unlock, drive outcomes.
- Academic imitation that adds terminology but no precision.

## Technical inflation

- Full setup before a narrow fix.
- Several equivalent implementations without a recommendation.
- Complete files where a small excerpt proves the change.
- Line-by-line narration of obvious code.
- Premature abstractions, compatibility sprawl, or defensive machinery.
- Generic best-practice annexes unrelated to the request.
- Comments that restate code.

## Caution inflation

- Generic disclaimers repeated around the answer.
- Professional-consultation boilerplate without a threshold for escalation.
- Limitation dumping before useful guidance.
- The same risk stated several times.
- Treating established facts and speculation with equal uncertainty.
- "Ultimately the decision is yours" when no real gate exists.

## Formatting load

- Bold text everywhere.
- One-line paragraphs used for artificial drama.
- Excessive whitespace, headings, labels, icons, or callout boxes.
- A table that repeats prose across columns.
- Quoting the prompt back to the Handler.
- Parenthetical explanations nested inside already qualified sentences.

## Final discriminator

Remove the element mentally. If no claim, evidence, action, necessary
qualification, distinction, or deliberate tone disappears, it is load.
